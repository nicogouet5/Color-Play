Compare the three in-store deployment stages.

[A] Immediate Discovery Gate (A1–A2) · Zero Tech Integration

Portable human-assisted comparison beside the rack, with zero data capture.

Before investing in store hardware or IT approvals, Stage 1 tests whether explained color guidance changes real garment selection at the rack. A trained specialist uses physical fabric drapes and a curated 6-garment rail to connect a seasonal palette to 2–3 tops in stock.

Physical Store Footprint [A]

0.8 m² movable garment rail + physical fabric swatch book + clip-on neutral CRI 95+ daylight lamp. No fixed wiring or POS connection.

In-Situ Shopper Flow [A]

Voluntary aisle invitation → 90-second physical fabric drape comparison → plain-language explanation → 2–3 tops pulled from the rack → free choice to try on or skip.

Data & Privacy Guardrail

Strict zero-capture protocol: no camera photos, no stored profiles, no email/phone collection, no CRM export, and no transaction linkage.

Decision Gate to Pass [A]

Gate A1: Continue only if ≥ 4/5 shoppers select or want to try a suggested garment, median trust ≥ 4/5, and ≤ 1 report feeling pressured.

Rack Signage Copy

“Unsure which color works beyond black or beige? Compare 3 tops on this rack — optional, 2 minutes, no photos.”

Station Hardware

Physical swatch drape board + printed reference card (no screen required during initial 5-shopper observation).

Merchandising Stock Rule

Pre-selected capsule of 6 in-stock tops across 3 color families, verified at shift start.

No-Result Fallback

If store lighting is mixed or shopper disagrees, hand over familiar neutral options with zero friction.

[A] Conditional Recommendation (Deliverable 6) · Maximum Learning per Dependency

A dedicated rack-side touch station that connects explained palettes to live store stock.

Recommended in Deliverable 6 once A1–A3 pass: a compact fixture mounted directly to a featured apparel rack. It combines calibrated neutral illumination, physical fabric comparison tabs, and an interactive touch screen that points the shopper to 2–3 exact hooks and available sizes on that rack.

Physical Store Footprint [A]

1.2 m² end-cap or feature rack bay + 12" angled touch terminal + upper CRI 95+ daylight canopy + 4 physical fabric comparison drapes.

In-Situ Shopper Flow [A]

Tap to start at the rack (no login) → compare physical drape under neutral light → read plain-language palette rationale → pick 1–3 tops from Hooks 01–06 → take ephemeral rack pass to fitting room.

Data & Privacy Guardrail

Ephemeral session only: no facial biometric storage and no mandatory account creation. Screen auto-resets 15 seconds after garment selection or exit.

Decision Gate to Pass [A]

Gates A2–A5: ≥ 80% agreement with two independent color specialists, ≤ 15 pp coverage gap, written Operations owner, and viable unit contribution.

Rack Signage Copy

“Test color clarity at the rack. See why 3 available tops work for your contrast — or skip anytime.”

Station Hardware

12" anti-glare touch display (45° ergonomic tilt) + physical fabric swatch bar mounted below the bezel.

Merchandising Stock Rule

Hooks 01–06 mapped to live store SKU availability; out-of-stock cuts are suppressed before session start.

No-Result Fallback

First-class “Unclear / No Result” button routes to human associate drape check or clean exit without forced advice.

[A] Cross-Category Advocacy Hypothesis · Unvalidated Expansion Scope

Apparel owns the initial purchase; Makeup / Beauty champions the cross-category opportunity.

In the v12 Decision-Making Unit [A], Makeup / Beauty acts as the internal champion because undertone and contrast guidance naturally bridges clothing and cosmetics. However, apparel remains the first commercial scope: shade matching and cross-category demand require a separate validation gate.

Physical Store Footprint [A]

Apparel station positioned on the primary boundary aisle between Women’s Apparel and the Beauty / Cosmetics floor.

In-Situ Shopper Flow [A]

Complete apparel rack comparison first → optional undertone takeaway card (warm/cool/neutral-soft) to discuss with a Beauty Counter specialist.

Scope Guardrail

No automated shade-matching camera claims and no cross-division profile database in the initial pilot.

Commercial & DMU Gate [A]

Appendix F Gate: Explicit Makeup champion advocacy and specialist access, while commercial budget signatory remains with Apparel Buying + Finance [A].

Aisle Bridge Copy

“Apparel first: find your garment palette here. Ask our Beauty counter how your undertone translates to lip & cheek families [A].”

Station Hardware

Apparel rack station with optional printed undertone bridge card (no automated cosmetic shade scanner).

Merchandising Stock Rule

100% anchored in available tops/shirts on the apparel rack; zero unverified cosmetic SKU claims.

Quality Boundary

Cosmetic shade matching requires in-person testing at the beauty counter after apparel validation.

Apparel Merchandising / Buying

[A] Proposed Buyer · Commercial Owner

What they see in-situ: A merchandising-first fixture that directs shoppers to full-price seasonal tops already hanging on Hooks 01–06—not an abstract styling app.

Decision Gate (A3 & A5): Written conditional pilot commitment naming budget owner and spending cap. Historical CLP 580 revenue vs. CLP 250 variable and CLP 660,000 fixed cost (breakeven 2,000 sessions vs. 660 capacity = −CLP 442,200/month) remains an unviable historical scenario only; real quotes and service times come first.

Makeup / Beauty Division

[A] Proposed Champion · Internal Advocate

What they see in-situ: A shared undertone and contrast vocabulary at the apparel rack that can later bridge shoppers to beauty counter consultations once apparel gates pass.

Scope Guardrail (Appendix F): Advocacy is not purchase authority. Apparel is the first commercial scope; cosmetic shade-matching accuracy and cross-category demand remain unvalidated [A].

Fast-Fashion Shopper (Ages 18–28)

[O] Survey Baseline (n=9) · [A] Behavioral Gate

What they see in-situ: Optional 2-minute fabric comparison with plain-language explanations, 2–3 immediate tops on the rack, zero photo capture, and a stress-free exit.

Decision Gate (A1 & A2): Among n=9 qualifying survey respondents, 8/9 rate usefulness highly and 9/9 report try-on intent [O, E2–E3]. Next test: observe 5 shoppers at a portable rack (≥4/5 select/try, median trust ≥4/5, ≤1 pressure report).
