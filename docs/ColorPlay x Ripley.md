## 1. The pitch in 30 seconds

> Ripley buys apparel 6–9 months ahead, blind to what shoppers *almost* bought. Color-Play is a computer-vision kiosk at the rack: it reads each shopper's colorimetry and tastes, tells her what suits her and where it is, and sends the data (linked by RUT) into Ripley's CRM. **She gets clarity; Merchants get better buys.** We sell the system to Ripley, then run it from inside.

**Tagline (deck cover):** *Color clarity at the rack. Better buys for Ripley.*

## 2. Strategy breakdown

### 2.1 Who pays, who benefits
| Role | Who | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic buyer (pays)** | Ripley **Merchants** | More accurate supplier buys by color × size × style → fewer liquidaciones, fewer lost sales |
| **Daily user** | The shopper (fast-fashion, 18–28 in our survey) | Confidence in color, a guided path through the store, a discount at caja |
| **Secondary stakeholders** `[A]` | Store operations, IT / Data, CRM & loyalty (Puntos Go), Legal / Privacy | Placement and staffing, integration, data loop, Law 21.719 compliance |

### 2.2 Problem (what we say)
1. Merchants commit supplier buys 6–9 months ahead `[A]`.
2. The signal they have is **what sold**, not what shoppers **doubted and dropped**. 78% of surveyed shoppers have dropped a garment over color doubt `[O]`.
3. The result: overstock → liquidaciones, and stockouts → lost sales. Inventory distortion ≈ 6.8% of retail sales `[S]` IHL Group 2024.

### 2.3 Solution (what we show)
- **Mode 1 (first visit):** RUT + consent → CV colorimetry (5000K light, no photo stored) → taste survey → live in-store recommendation with location → ticket/QR → caja.
- **Mode 2 (returning):** RUT only → saved colors, sizes, tastes → straight to the aisle → same ticket/QR.
- **Data loop:** kiosk session (RUT) → caja scan (what she bought) → Ripley CRM → Merchants.

### 2.4 Why Ripley wins
| For the shopper | For Merchants |
|---|---|
| 100% would try the garments suggested `[O]` | Color × size × taste demand signal tied to real purchases |
| 89% found the guidance useful `[O]` | Better supplier-buy accuracy per store |
| Cumulative discount + Puntos Go sign-up | Loyalty and CRM enrichment as a by-product |

### 2.5 Positioning and competition
Two groups exist today; **nobody serves both sides of the same data loop**.

| | Helps the shopper | Helps the Merchant |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical alternatives** | Color-analysis apps, virtual try-on, stylist tools | Demand-forecasting and merchandise-planning software |
| **Color-Play** | ✔ in-store, real-time | ✔ same session data, linked to purchase |

Three differentiators: (1) **in-store at the rack**, not an app; (2) **RUT-linked** to the CRM so it closes the loop with caja; (3) **built for Ripley's Merchants**, not for generic retail.
*(Competitor names: add after desk research; none are quoted in the deck.)*

### 2.6 Business model — sell, then embed
| Step | What | Amount `[A]` |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pilot | 6 months, 1 store, 1 category | CLP 25 MM |
| 2. Sale | System + IP; 80 upfront + 120 earn-out when pilot KPIs are met | CLP 200 MM |
| 3. Embedded team | 3 people in Ripley's UX/UI/data area, monthly fee | CLP 12 MM / month |

Team revenue over 5 years: **CLP 801 MM** (base). Ripley's base-case cumulative net value: **+CLP 750 MM, payback in year 4**. Conservative **−189**, optimistic **+1,689**. Break-even: trim ≥ 2.6% of apparel inventory distortion. Full inputs: alignment file §5.

**IP caution:** under Ley 19.039 Art. 37 the CV algorithm alone is hard to patent in Chile. We protect via copyright (Ley 17.336), industrial design / utility model for the rack-kiosk, and contract transfer of data model, UI and brand. Do not say "patents" in the pitch until an IP lawyer / INAPI confirms.

### 2.7 Go-to-market
1. **Discovery (4 weeks):** meet Merchants, validate pain, agree KPIs. Cost ≈ CLP 1.2 MM.
2. **Pilot:** one store, one category, portable rack.
3. **Decision gate:** earn-out tied to sell-through lift, ticket uptake, in-stock accuracy.
4. **Rollout:** 1 → 10 → 25 → 41 stores across years 1–4.

### 2.8 Risks and how we answer them
| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| "n = 9 is tiny" | Said openly: exploratory survey, percentages only, `[A]` labels; the pilot is the real test |
| Privacy (RUT-first) | Explicit consent, no photos stored, retention rule, Law 21.719 in force 1 Dec 2026 |
| "Will shoppers actually use it?" | Ticket/QR discount + Puntos Go; Mode 2 removes friction for repeat users |
| Conservative case does not pay back | Pilot gate + earn-out structure shifts risk to our side |
| IP not patentable | Layered protection above |
| Ripley builds it internally | Sell-then-embed: we become the internal team |

## 3. Brand breakdown

### 3.1 Co-brand logic
The deck is **Color-Play's proposal to Ripley**, so Ripley's identity is the frame and Color-Play is the guest.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Top bar (every slide) | Left: chip with **Color-Play logo | Simple Tech / UDD logo**. Right: label **"PROPOSAL FOR"** + **Ripley logo** |
| Top stripe | Ripley four-color stripe (plum · yellow · red · grey) |
| Ripley logo | Never recolored, stretched or placed on clashing backgrounds. On dark backgrounds use a dark pill/chip behind it (as on the cover) |
| Color-Play logo | Always on a light chip; keep clear space equal to the mark's height |
| Simple Tech / UDD | Secondary, always next to the Color-Play logo, never larger |

### 3.2 Color
| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ripley plum | `#741472` | Primary headings, key shapes, stripe |
| Ripley yellow | `#ffd400` | Highlights, stripe, CTA accents |
| Ripley red | `#c8102e` | Stripe, problem/risk emphasis |
| Ripley grey | `#cfd4d8` | Stripe, dividers, quiet backgrounds |
| Color-Play magenta | `#a8115f` | Color-Play emphasis, charts |
| Color-Play pink | `#e6338a` | Accent, active states |
| Soft pink | `#f7a3cf` | Tints, backgrounds of badges |
| Dark | `#16121c` | Text on light, dark slides |

Rule of thumb: **plum/yellow/red/grey = Ripley's world; magenta/pink = Color-Play's world.** Keep them in separate zones of a slide so the co-brand stays readable.

### 3.3 Typography
- **Manrope** — headings and body (bold for headlines, regular/medium for text).
- **JetBrains Mono** — only for the ticket / code-like elements (`CP-ALC-84920`).
- Headlines are **one sentence that states the message** (action titles), not topic labels.

### 3.4 Slide system
- 1920 × 1080, one idea per slide, ≤ 3 text blocks, the visual carries the story.
- Percentages large, source small. `n=9` only in speaker notes.
- Every `[A]` figure carries an "assumption to validate" footer.
- Visual priority: **real renders → real kiosk UI → ticket/QR → simple charts**.

### 3.5 Voice and tone
- **Warm, clear, retail-literate.** Speak the Merchant's language (OTB, liquidaciones, traspasos, caja) and the shopper's (clarity, confidence).
- Short sentences, verbs first, no jargon about computer vision on the shopper side.
- Honest about uncertainty: "exploratory", "assumption", "to validate".
- Avoid superlatives and unproven claims ("guaranteed", "AI-powered revolution").

### 3.6 Visual assets
| Asset | Where it is used |
|---|---|
| 3D store render | Cover, status slide |
| Kiosk close-up render | Value proposition |
| Portable rack render | Go-to-market |
| Live-prototype screens (welcome, taste, result) | How it works |
| Paper ticket + QR (to scan at caja) | How it works, value |
| Color-Play, Simple Tech/UDD and Ripley logos | Header on every slide |
